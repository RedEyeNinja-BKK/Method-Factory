## What “good” looks like

A useful store review should **help you make better selling decisions**, not give you a giant list of opinions.

The right package is a **prioritized improvement plan** built around:

1. **What you sell**
2. **Who is likely to buy it**
3. **Why someone would choose you**
4. **Where shoppers may hesitate or leave**
5. **Which changes are most likely to improve views, clicks, trust, and purchases**
6. **What to test first—and how we will know whether it helped**

### The deliverable should be

#### 1. A clear store diagnosis

In plain language:

- What is already working
- What is weakening buyer confidence
- What makes the shop confusing, generic, or easy to overlook
- Whether the products, pricing, photos, descriptions, policies, and branding support the same buyer promise
- Whether the shop looks trustworthy and professionally maintained

#### 2. A ranked action list

Not “fix everything.” Something like:

| Priority | Change | Why it matters | Effort | How to measure it |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Improve the first image on three listings | Determines whether shoppers click | Low | Listing views → visits |
| 2 | Rewrite titles around buyer language | Improves relevance and clarity | Medium | Search impressions/clicks |
| 3 | Clarify processing and delivery expectations | Reduces purchase hesitation | Low | Favorites/carts/orders |
| 4 | Test a stronger product bundle | Raises order value | Medium | Conversion and average order |

You should finish knowing **exactly what to do this week**, not merely what is “wrong.”

#### 3. Practical rewrites and examples

Where useful, the review should include actual working material:

- Revised listing titles
- Improved opening descriptions
- Photo-order recommendations
- Better calls to action
- Shop announcement or About-section suggestions
- FAQ and policy wording
- Bundle, variation, or offer ideas
- Examples of stronger positioning

Advice should be specific to your products—not generic SEO filler.

#### 4. A small testing plan

Every major recommendation should be treated as a hypothesis:

> “If we make this change, we expect more shoppers to understand the product and take the next step.”

Then test a manageable number of changes, allowing enough time to observe results. The review should distinguish between:

- **Must fix**
- **Worth testing**
- **Optional polish**
- **Not worth your time right now**

#### 5. Protection against wasted effort

A good review should explicitly call out what **not** to do.

For example:

- Don’t rewrite every listing before identifying the strongest opportunities
- Don’t chase keywords that do not match buyer intent
- Don’t change prices without understanding margin and positioning
- Don’t add products just to make the shop look larger
- Don’t spend hours on branding details while the main listings are unclear
- Don’t judge success only by views, likes, or followers

## A sensible package structure

### Store Sales Review

**Phase 1 — Intake and baseline**

- Store link
- Product and customer context
- Current goals
- Available shop statistics
- Constraints such as time, budget, production capacity, and shipping limits

**Phase 2 — Store and listing review**

- Storefront
- Product selection
- Best and weakest listings
- Search presentation
- Photography
- Copy and buyer clarity
- Pricing and offer structure
- Trust signals
- Customer journey
- Competitor context where relevant

**Phase 3 — Action plan**

- Top 3–5 improvements
- Exact recommended changes
- Priority and effort estimates
- Test plan
- Suggested order of execution

**Phase 4 — Implementation support**

Optional, but valuable:

- Rewrite priority listings
- Help create or improve images
- Review changes before publishing
- Check results after a defined period
- Adjust based on evidence

## The success standard

The package is successful if, at the end, you can answer:

- **Who is this store for?**
- **What should I sell or emphasize first?**
- **Which listings deserve my time?**
- **What should I change immediately?**
- **What should I leave alone?**
- **How will I know whether the changes worked?**

It should reduce uncertainty and give you a short, realistic plan—not create another pile of homework.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> No honest review can guarantee more sales. It can, however, improve the quality of your offer, remove avoidable buyer friction, focus your effort on the highest-value changes, and create a way to learn from the results.
